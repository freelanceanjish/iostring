The Organisation

       

Zenith Education Network is a K-12 group spanning 48 schools across three Indian states, 28,000 students, 1,200+ teaching staff, and a leadership team that believed deeply in the power of education to change lives.

       

In 2023, they had a problem. The world had changed. Their classrooms had not.

       
             
  • Mixed-grade schools from Grade 1 to Grade 12
    •          
  • Urban and semi-urban campuses across Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Telangana
    •          
  • Established in 1988, a proud legacy and a complex bureaucracy
    •          
  • National and ICSE curriculum tracks running in parallel
    •        
     
     
             
   
       
     
       
         
The problem
         

COVID revealed the gap. Digital acceleration widened it.

         

When remote learning became mandatory in 2020, Zenith scrambled, deploying Zoom, Google Classroom, and a new LMS within weeks. Two years later, the tools were paid for. The adoption was not happening.

         

By 2023, teacher digital confidence was critically low. LMS usage was at 18%. Student engagement scores had dropped. Meanwhile, Zenith's competitors were pulling ahead with interactive digital content, personalised learning paths, and data-driven teaching.

         

The CEO's mandate was clear: transform in 18 months or lose a generation of students, and teachers, to better-equipped schools.

         
           
             
01
             

LMS adoption at 18%, teachers uploaded resources but did not use interactive features, gradebooks, or parent communication modules.

           
           
             
02
             

Teacher resistance was high, 62% of staff in a baseline survey said they felt "unsupported" during previous technology rollouts. Trust in leadership was fragile.

           
           
             
03
             

Siloed decision-making, IT, academic leadership, HR, and school principals were not talking to each other. Every department had a different tool, a different vendor, a different priority.

           
           
             
04
             

No transformation metrics, progress was measured by budget spend and training session attendance. Nobody was tracking actual adoption or student impact.

           
         
       
       
                 
     
   
       
     
How we worked
     

Our approach: five phases, fourteen months.

     

We designed the engagement using an Agile Business Transformation framework, iterative, feedback-driven, people-first at every step.

     
       
         
01
         
           
Months 1–2 · Discovery Sprint
           

Listen Before You Lead

           

Our team spent the first six weeks on-site across five representative schools, attending classes, interviewing teachers, shadowing principals, and analysing six months of LMS log data. We did not pitch a solution in week one. We listened.

           

Outcome: A 48-page Digital Readiness Report that mapped four distinct teacher personas, identified three critical technology friction points, and gave the leadership team honest data for the first time.

           
                                       
         
       
       
         
02
         
           
Months 2–3 · Strategy Design
           

Co-Create, Don't Dictate

           

We ran a two-day leadership offsite with Zenith's CEO, COO, four regional heads, and six school principals, using Lean Canvas and OKR workshops to align on a transformation vision everyone owned, not just the one the CTO had written in a slide deck.

           

Outcome: A 14-month Agile Business Transformation Roadmap with clear OKRs, stream-aligned teams for digital delivery, and a "Digital Champion" programme that embedded change agents in every school.

         
       
       
         
03
         
           
Months 3–8 · Launch & Embed
           

Sprints in the Classroom

           

We trained 48 Digital Champions, one per school, using a two-week Agile Facilitation bootcamp. Each champion led their school through six-week adoption sprints for each technology module: the LMS gradebook, interactive content creation, and the parent communication portal.

           

Our coaches ran weekly retrospectives with each school cohort, identifying blockers, celebrating wins, and adjusting the adoption plan in real time. No two schools ran the same sprint plan.

           
                                       
         
       
       
         
04
         
           
Months 8–12 · Scale & Deepen
           

From Adoption to Mastery

           

Phase three moved beyond tool adoption into pedagogical transformation. We introduced "Learning Squads", cross-school teams of teachers who collaboratively designed digital lesson plans, shared data insights, and peer-coached each other on differentiated instruction using the new platform.

           

Zenith's first digital parents' evening, streamed live to 8,000 households, happened in month eleven. It was built entirely by the Learning Squads, with no external coaching involvement.

           
                                       
         
       
       
         
05
         
           
Months 12–14 · Sustain & Exit
           

Building a Transformation That Outlasts Us

           

The final phase was entirely about sustainability. We built Zenith's internal Agile Centre of Excellence, a team of six certified Agile Practitioners recruited and trained from within. We ran a two-day "Coaches' Handoff" with the leadership team, documenting playbooks, retrospective templates, and the OKR measurement framework.

           

Outcome: By month 14, our coaches had stepped back from all 48 schools. Zenith's internal team ran the next quarterly planning cycle entirely independently, and scored it a 4.6 out of 5 in their own retrospective.

What this engagement taught us

     
       
         
01
         

Trust is the technology. In education, adoption blockers are almost never technical. They are relational. The moment a teacher trusted their Digital Champion, their LMS usage tripled.

       
       
         
02
         

Sprint cadence works in classrooms. Six-week adoption cycles with retrospectives gave teachers a safe, predictable rhythm for change, the same principle that works in product teams works in pedagogy.

       
       
         
03
         

Exit strategy starts on day one. Every coaching decision was made with sustainability in mind. We turned teachers into coaches, not dependents. That is the only transformation that lasts.

The new frontier
     

Your teams adopted AI.
Nobody redesigned how humans work with it.
